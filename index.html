Project Overview, Charter & Scope

Business need

Southbank Institute of Technology (a fictional university) runs four separate student portals: Enrolment & Records, Fees & Payments, Timetable, and Student Services Bookings. Students need four logins and see conflicting data, and the service desk handles about 900 “where do I find…” tickets a month. Vendor support for the Fees portal ends on 30 September 2026, so the four portals must be consolidated into one secure, accessible portal with single sign-on (SSO) by that date.

Objectives

  1. Replace four legacy portals with one MyUni portal and one SSO login by 28 Sep 2026.
  2. Complete the project by 30 Sep 2026 within the total budget of $177,940.
  3. Migrate active student records with ≥99.5% reconciliation accuracy and ≤0.5% duplicates.
  4. Comply with the Privacy Act 1988 (APPs) and WCAG 2.2 AA accessibility.
  5. Keep enrolment and fees services available, with no unplanned outage over 4 hours during cutover.

Development approach: hybrid

Governance, data migration, testing and cutover are predictive because they have fixed dependencies, a fixed deadline and compliance sign-offs. The portal is built in four short agile sprints, so students and staff see working modules at every sprint review and can shape the design. The sponsor gets a firm baseline, and the product can still respond to user feedback.

Scope control

The scope statement and WBS form the scope baseline. Any change is logged, assessed for its impact on schedule, cost, quality and risk, and decided by the Steering Committee. For example, CR-01 (add the Careers portal) was rejected and moved to a Phase 2 backlog, because it would have delayed go-live by four days and missed the contract deadline.

Stakeholder analysis

We scored 14 stakeholders for power and interest (1–5, where 3 or more counts as high). The Student Association sits at power 3 (it shapes student opinion and adoption) and interest 5 (it represents the portal's users), so it is managed closely through co-design workshops and sprint demos. Individual students have high interest but low power, so they are kept informed with emails, banners and guides.

Scope → Schedule → CostThe charter scope becomes the WBS. WBS work packages become ProjectLibre tasks. Task durations × resource rates give the cost baseline and PV.
Tracking → DashboardTrello cards carry WBS codes. Their % complete feeds ProjectLibre and the EV calculation in Excel, which drives the dashboard.
Risk → Issue → ChangeR01 occurred and became I-01. The corrective action became CR-04, funded from the contingency reserve in the budget.
Stakeholders → CommunicationPower/interest scores set each stakeholder's engagement level, which sets the method and frequency in the communication plan.
Quality → ControlOff-target metrics become issues; the Go/No-Go uses quality thresholds; the RACI gives each decision one accountable owner.

Recommendation: proceed to the 28 September go-live, with conditions

The project is viable. It is 4 percentage points behind plan (SPI 0.94) but close to budget (CPI 0.98), and the forecast cost of $157,242 is within the budget plus contingency ($170,203). The delay sits on the near-critical data path and has already been corrected.

  1. Condition 1: the dress rehearsal (3.5, 22 Sep) reaches ≥99.5% reconciliation and ≤0.5% duplicates.
  2. Condition 2: the Finance API fix is confirmed by 22 Sep. If not, go live with the fees module still linked to the legacy Fees portal (phased go-live).
  3. Condition 3: zero open Critical/High defects at the Go/No-Go meeting on 25 Sep.

Unresolved: the Careers portal and SMS notifications (Phase 2); benefits to be measured over 3–12 months after go-live. If Condition 1 fails, the Steering Committee should delay go-live rather than migrate poor-quality data. Service continuity and privacy matter more than the date.
